Greenhouse is one of the most widely used Applicant Tracking Systems in tech. If you are applying to startups and mid-size companies, there is a high chance your application flows through Greenhouse. Understanding how it works from the recruiter side gives you a massive advantage.

## How Greenhouse Scores Candidates

When a recruiter sets up a job in Greenhouse, they define a scorecard. This scorecard contains the attributes they will rate each candidate on — typically things like "JavaScript proficiency," "system design," or "culture fit." Each attribute gets a weight, and interviewers submit scores after each stage.

<Callout variant="tip">
  **Pro tip:** Many Greenhouse instances are configured to auto-reject candidates who score below a threshold on knockout questions. Always triple-check your answers to "Do you have X years of experience?" type questions.
</Callout>

The scoring system is not just subjective — Greenhouse aggregates scores across interviewers and stages, producing a ranked list. Recruiters rarely look beyond the top 10-15 candidates. This means you need to perform consistently well across all interview stages, not just nail the technical portion.

## Knockout Questions and Auto-Rejection

Greenhouse allows recruiters to add knockout questions at the application stage. These are binary filters: answer wrong, and your application never reaches a human. Common knockout questions include:

- Work authorization status
- Minimum years of experience with a specific technology
- Willingness to relocate or work specific hours
- Salary expectations within a predefined range

<Callout variant="warning">
  Never lie on knockout questions. Greenhouse logs all answers, and if you get caught misrepresenting your experience during later stages, you will be permanently blacklisted from that company.
</Callout>

## The Recruiter Dashboard

Recruiters see applications in a pipeline view: Applied → Phone Screen → Technical Interview → Onsite → Offer. Each stage has a conversion rate, and Greenhouse tracks time-in-stage metrics. If your application has been sitting in "Applied" for two weeks without movement, it is likely deprioritized or the role has been filled internally.
