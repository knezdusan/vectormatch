React is still the most in-demand frontend skill in 2026, but the shape of the job has changed. "React developer" is no longer a role on its own — it is a baseline. What separates a callback from a rejection now is the cluster of adjacent skills hiring teams expect to see alongside it.

<TechStack technologies={["React", "Next.js", "TypeScript", "Tailwind", "GraphQL"]} />

## The skills that now travel with React

When we analyze live ATS postings, the pattern is consistent: the highest-volume React roles assume you also own the rendering strategy, the type system, and at least part of the data layer. Job descriptions that used to say "experience with React" now enumerate Server Components, streaming, and edge rendering.

<Callout variant="note">
  The single biggest shift since 2024 is the move from client-heavy SPAs to server-first frameworks. Roles mentioning **React Server Components** or **Next.js App Router** grew far faster than generic "React" postings.
</Callout>

This means the way you describe your experience matters. A resume that lists "React, Redux, REST" reads as 2021. A resume that demonstrates "React Server Components, TypeScript, and incremental data fetching" reads as someone who can ship today.

## Where the roles actually live

Most candidates still hunt on the large job boards, where a single React opening attracts hundreds of applicants within hours. The better-signal openings flow directly through ATS platforms before they are ever syndicated.

<ArticleCard slug="ats-vs-linkedin" />

If you understand which platform a company uses, you can apply early and tailor your application to how that system parses and ranks candidates.

## Specializing without pigeonholing

The market rewards a clear primary specialization paired with credible range. Pick the lane your experience actually supports — performance, design systems, data-heavy dashboards, or platform/tooling — and make it unambiguous.

<Callout variant="warning">
  Avoid listing ten frameworks you touched once. Knockout filters and recruiters both penalize unfocused profiles. Depth in a coherent stack beats a long, shallow list.
</Callout>

## What to do this quarter

- Rebuild one portfolio project on a server-first framework and write up the tradeoffs.
- Convert a meaningful codebase to strict TypeScript and document what it caught.
- Quantify impact: render times, bundle size, conversion, or reliability numbers.

The developers getting offers in 2026 are not the ones who know the most libraries. They are the ones who can show judgment about when to reach for the platform versus the client, and who get in front of the right openings before the crowd does.
